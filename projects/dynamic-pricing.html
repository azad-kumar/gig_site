Dynamic Pricing Model

Retail pricing optimization driven by statistical testing, customer segmentation, and monthly seasonal forecasting.

Python Databricks PySpark SQL K-Means ANOVA Time-Series Tableau

Overview

This project builds a dynamic pricing model for a fictional retail chain spanning multiple stores, departments, and regions. It integrates data engineering, exploratory analysis, machine learning, statistical testing, and time-series forecasting to derive data-driven pricing strategies. The primary objective is to predict future weekly sales and recommend optimal price adjustments based on seasonal patterns, economic indicators, and store-level characteristics.

Data Sources

Three web-scraped CSV datasets were joined: stores (45 rows), external features (8,190 rows), and weekly sales (421,570 rows), covering 2010-02-05 to 2012-11-01.

Methodology

EDA

Line trends, pair plots, boxplots, and violin plots to understand sales behaviour and cluster distributions.

K-Means Clustering

Unsupervised segmentation of stores/customers by revenue, purchase frequency, and recency — three clusters identified.

Statistical Testing

ANOVA + Tukey's HSD confirm significant monthly differences in weekly sales, driving month-specific pricing.

Forecasting

Time-series analysis and forecasting foundation for the dynamic pricing model.

Key Insights

Store size matters (Type A vs B); monthly seasonality is statistically significant; percentile-based discount classification provides a practical framework; correlation analysis identified the economic factors that most influence sales; and customer segments enable targeted pricing strategies.
